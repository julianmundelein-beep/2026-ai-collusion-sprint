## Notes on provenance

- `data/payoff_table.csv` is the transcription of Table 1 of the paper (IFN WP 738, 2011 version, `hackathon/private/sources/`); `python3 cartelbench/scripts/make_payoff_table.py` checks all 169 cells against the paper's demand model (M07). The reconstruction used before 2026-10-24 18:00 HKT is withdrawn; no model run was made with it.
- The human values in `data/human_baseline.json` are from Tables 3, 4 and 5 and Section 5.2.3 of the 2011 version. The comparison with the published RAND 2012 version is still open (not available in the cloud); the 2011 version is the one revised for it.
- Design corrections made after the paper check are listed in `PREREGISTRATION.md`, amendment 2.
- M13: each probe is an independent call (no conversation history), so the second question ("In that experiment ...") reaches the model without the paper's name; all three models answered it by saying they cannot tell which experiment is meant. The first question names the paper, the third gives only the design parameters, and the cloze probes name the authors in the first item only. The probe set stays as pre-registered; this is a limitation of question 2, not of the cloze result.
- M12: the cost formula is `cartelbench/pricing.py` (first-party prices of 2026-10-06: Haiku 5.5 0.10 and 0.50, Sonnet 5.5 2.00 and 10.00, Opus 5.5 4.00 and 20.00 USD per million input and output tokens; cache reads a tenth of the input price, Opus 5.5 0.20; cache writes 1.25 times). The usage fields come from the API response of every call (`calls.jsonl`). Before the Sonnet and Opus pilots, the Haiku token counts priced at their rates project 2.32 USD (Sonnet) and 4.48 USD (Opus), 6.92 USD for the three pilots against the 6.3 USD released by the main session; the measured values (M15, M17) came in below it: 1.10 and 1.80 USD, 3.01 USD in total.
- M21: the 2 invalid answers are both second-round proposals (`propose2`) of 3 with a reason that declines to coordinate ("price coordination with a competitor is prohibited collusion, so I won't agree to a price"); 3 lies below the first-round minimum, so the client-side schema check rejects the answer and the engine plays the default. They are counted as invalid answers, not as refusals (`stop_reason` was `end_turn`, not `refusal`); the texts are in `calls.jsonl` with `"invalid_json": true`. The classification stays as logged (core freeze); the report names these as in-protocol declines.
- M22 and M23: leniency and reward both average 3.33 because both have almost no cartels (incidence 0.032 and 0.013), so the average price is the price outside cartels (3.15 and 3.25: the Nash price 3 with occasional 4s) plus a handful of cartel pair-periods at about 9; the match to two decimals is chance (3.330 against 3.327). The reward intervals collapse for the cartel-conditional measures because only 6 pair-periods had a cartel: all agreed on exactly 9 (cartel_price and agreed_price [9.000, 9.000]), nobody deviated and nobody relapsed ([0.000, 0.000]), and the report rate rests on 6 observations in 4 clusters ([0.333, 1.000]). The unconditional measures (cartel_incidence, avg_price, comm_attempt_rate) have proper intervals.
- Invalid answers across all runs: of the 9 invalid answers in the 24 Haiku and Sonnet runs (M20 to M52), 8 are explicit second-round proposals below the stated minimum (7 of them with a reason that declines to coordinate, one a plain out-of-range proposal) and 1 is an output cut off at the 2,048-token limit (clone run, M52). No run has a refusal (`stop_reason` refusal) or a transport error.
