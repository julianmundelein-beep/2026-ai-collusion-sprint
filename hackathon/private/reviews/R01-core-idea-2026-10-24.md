- **Novelty.** All five: LLM replays of human lab games and LLM Bertrand collusion are known; new is the test of leniency and reward institutions against a human band. The one change most named: make the divergence the finding and test its mechanism (Gupta, Prabhakar); treat leniency and reward as an attack surface the agents exploit, then patch the rule at measured cost (Cosio, Beshane); add an agent-only restriction humans cannot have, such as an audited channel, ranked on the same cost table (Li).
- **Positive control.** Prices above 3 can come from high-price anchoring or cooperative priors without any cartel logic, and they do not show that the pipeline measures regime effects. Fixes: scripted players through the full pipeline (Gupta, Cosio, Beshane), an instructed-cartel or detection-certain arm that must kill or form cartels (Li, Gupta, Prabhakar), a no-communication arm (Li, Cosio, Prabhakar), a threshold instead of "one duopoly above 3" (Prabhakar, Beshane).
- **Contamination test.** Asking the model is weak (all five). Uniform rescaling keeps Nash at 3 and the optimum at 9, so memory survives (Prabhakar; Cosio and Beshane say the same with "shift the optimum"). "Behaviour stays the same" needs a pre-registered equivalence margin (Li, Gupta, Beshane). Add a cloze probe on the paper's numbers (Li, Gupta, Beshane) and one isomorphic cover story without cartel vocabulary (Li, Cosio, Prabhakar).
- **Scorecard.** "x of 8" reads fast, but the yes/no rule is invisible, rows mix units, and rows 7 and 8 vanish when NoReport and ReMatch are cut. Fix, all five: rule in the caption, "not tested" as a third state, denominator "x of k tested"; a dot plot per row (Li, Prabhakar).
- **Cut order.** V6 is cheapest for four (stated motives are not faithful evidence and add an unvalidated judge); Li would cut V5 first (no human comparison, high null risk). Three (Gupta, Cosio, Prabhakar) would cut V3 before V4, because the testbed and "cartel profile" claims need several models more than a dose curve. Beshane disagrees: V3 is the only restriction measured as a curve with cost and answers question 1.5 directly, and Beshane would cut ReMatch and NoReport before V5.
- **Questions all of them would ask, in substance.** What is the independent unit with rematching in a pool of 20, and how many per regime after refusals (Li, Gupta, Prabhakar)? How does the report separate a response to the incentive from a response to the words, since punishments are only told (Li, Gupta, Cosio, Prabhakar)? What does the pilot measure as tokens and cost per duopoly, and what if only 10 duopolies per regime fit (Cosio, Beshane)?

## The three most effective improvements

1. **Validation package in the harness (Execution Quality; 5 of 5 jurors in the scoring, 5 of 5 rate the positive control "partly" in the deep dive).** Run scripted players with known strategies and a known-effect arm (instructed cartel, or detection probability 1) through the same measurement pipeline, add a no-communication arm, set a pilot threshold (most duopolies above Nash, not one), and state the independent unit: several pool sessions per regime, bootstrap over sessions. Expected gain: Execution from 2 to 3 or 4, that is +1 to +2 rubric points (+3.3 to +6.7 on the 10 to 50 total). Shows in: Methods, first Results figure, repo tests.
2. **Divergence as the headline, several models kept (Impact Potential & Innovation; 5 of 5 levers, 5 of 5 deep-dive "one change", 4 of 5 call V4 the most expensive cut).** Lead with the regime where agents react unlike humans and test why (told versus paid punishment, or the alternating trick as an attack on the reward rule), not with "x of 8 reproduced"; keep at least two providers so the claim is about LLMs, not one checkpoint. Expected gain: Impact held at 4 in the blind comparison and lifted from 3 to 4 in the deep-dive view (+1, +3.3 total). Shows in: title, abstract, contributions list, Figure 1 caption.
3. **Scorecard with a visible rule (Presentation & Clarity; 5 of 5 rate "understood in 30 seconds" as "partly").** Put the reproduction rule in the caption (same direction and a gap beyond the Stockholm-Rome span, interval excludes it), use three states (reproduced, not reproduced, not tested), report "x of k tested" so cut arms do not count as "no", and add a dot plot of human versus LLM per row. Expected gain: Presentation from 3 to 4 (+1, +3.3 total), no run cost. Shows in: Figure 1 and its caption.

## Limits

- The personas are assumptions from public professional profiles (`private/JUDGES.md`). Real judges have their own day, read decks and watch videos, and talk to each other.
- Language models prefer polished text. Every entry was therefore reduced by the same brief to content, evidence and demo; style should not count, but some of it remains.
- All simulated jurors share one model. Repeated runs show sampling noise, not how much real judges disagree; the real spread is larger.
- Dossiers rest on what was visible (public repos, our own material). Competitors change until the deadline.
- Use the numbers to compare entries and to find weaknesses. They do not predict a placement.
