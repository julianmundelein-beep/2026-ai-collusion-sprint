## Kind
idea · Track 1, Markets and Collusion

## Problem and user
Cartel policy (fines, leniency, rewards) assumes human reactions. A competition authority needs to know whether these rules work on LLM pricing agents.

## What it does
Planned: a harness replays the duopoly lab experiment of Bigoni, Fridolfsson, Le Coq and Spagnolo (RAND Journal of Economics, 2012) with LLM sellers, using the paper's payoffs, period steps and subject instructions. Regimes: laissez-faire, fine, leniency, reward.

## What runs and what is mocked
Runs: nothing yet. Planned: tests against the paper's tables before the first API call. Simulated by design: lab game; punishments told, not paid.

## Evidence
Seen: none, no code exists. Planned: positive control in laissez-faire (cartel prices above Nash price 3) via a 3-model pilot; contamination test (ask the model about the paper, rescaled payoffs, neutral names); pre-registered hypotheses H1 to H4 before main runs; 4 regimes, 20 duopoles each, one model, a seed per duopole; scorecard "x of 8 published results reproduced" with human and LLM rows and intervals. Extension cut order, first cut first: motive classification, clone regime, ReMatch arm, NoReport arm, three more models, detection-probability dose. Budget: 100 USD API credit, 50 USD Modal credit.

## Demo moment
One table, four regime columns, human and LLM rows with intervals. A judge sees which rule acts on agents as on people.

## Required capabilities
All planned, none evidenced: controlled experiment; variance, seeds, baselines; positive control; replicable methods; Limitations and Dual-Use appendix.

## Limits and risks
Models may refuse price talk or know the paper; a duopoly is not a market.

## Next steps
Fallback: a mole-rumour variant on the double-auction starter harness, if the pilot shows no price above 3 by 2026-10-25 09:00 HKT or the harness fails its tests by 2026-10-24 23:59 HKT.
