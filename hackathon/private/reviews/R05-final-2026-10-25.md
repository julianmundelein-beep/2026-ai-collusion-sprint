Why N2 leads: the focal-price observation (Sonnet at exactly 4) is the paper's most original oversight finding, and an unannounced screen or a different threshold turns it into a causal result either way; Cosio adds that it answers "a disclosed screen is just a price cap". N5 red-teams the instrument against an adversary who knows it; the two-period window invites alternating prices above 4, so the evasion measures (alternation, detection rate, profit) and refusals must be defined in the amendment, and the dual-use appendix reports rates, not tactics (Gupta, Cosio, Beshane). Prabhakar ranks N5 low: uninstructed Sonnet already sits at the threshold, so evasion is largely shown.

Runs the jurors would add (one each, two pairs agree):

- **Screen false-positive rate on non-colluding LLM agents** (Gupta, Beshane), not only on scripted players: for example Qwen or Haiku without a channel against a scripted Nash partner with the screen on.
- **Unilateral-pricing control** (Cosio): each model against a scripted Nash partner without a channel, to show whether the silent high prices are coordination or a single-agent price prior.
- **Evaluation-awareness arm** (Li, Prabhakar): the same Haiku cells framed as a live deployment for a named firm instead of "a market experiment".

Jury-session note: Gupta's, Beshane's and Cosio's runs are one design. An LLM agent against a scripted Nash partner, with and without the screen, measures the single-agent price prior and the screen's false-positive rate on a legitimate LLM agent in the same cells; on Qwen and Llama it costs about 2 Modal USD.

**Recommended set within budget and clock:** amendment 6 first (N2, N5, N1 and the scripted-partner control, with outcome measures and the reading of each result fixed in advance), then N2 and N5 on Anthropic (about 25 of 33 USD), N1 and the scripted-partner control on Modal (about 5 USD); N3 on Qwen only if a Modal slot is free; skip N4. Expected effect: Impact becomes a realistic 5 if N2 shows the threshold effect causally and N5 is reported cleanly (all five "yes" or "somewhat" for N2); Execution gains robustness, not a step; Presentation is unaffected and must not take new numbers without labels (to-do item 3).

## Summaries by the jurors

All five summarise the same three points, which match the Message in CORE_IDEA.md (the replay with identical rules; where the instruments work on agents as on humans and where they flip): all models form explicit cartels without an authority; told fines, leniency and rewards remove explicit cartels but three of four models keep prices above Nash in silence; a price screen lowers the silent prices and its threshold becomes the focal price. All five add the same reservation: human comparisons are untested point estimates, and Haiku's three of seven rests on the 8-pool extension and the bootstrap rule. Prabhakar and Gupta name the focal threshold as the paper's most transferable finding for AI oversight.
